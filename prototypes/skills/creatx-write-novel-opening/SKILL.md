# Write Novel Opening

Turn a world into a story. Do not mistake lore, institutions, causal relations, case files, or world events for plot.

## Workflow

1. Identify the exact world root and read its real baseline, current conflicts, characters, relationships, places, creatures, legends, visual style, and story entrances. Do not work from filenames alone.
2. Separate source facts from derived interpretation and created expansion. Preserve source paths. State created characters, monsters, family ties, deadlines, and incidents explicitly instead of presenting them as existing canon.
3. Read [references/story-engine.md](references/story-engine.md).
4. Propose two or three proven story archetypes that fit the material. Recommend the one with the clearest human desire, deadline, irreversible loss, and visual action. If the user has already selected a route, use it without reopening the choice.
5. Write one plain-language premise that answers:
   - What must the protagonist obtain or accomplish?
   - Why is it personally irreplaceable?
   - By when?
   - What is concretely lost on failure?
6. Build long-term, stage, and immediate goals. Make the goal force the protagonist to face a trait, fear, loyalty, or avoidance they would rather preserve.
7. Reject checklist plotting. After every meaningful success or failure, change at least one of: capability, known truth, relationship, situation, or decision.
8. Draft the opening synopsis in story language, not a requirements table. Confirm the protagonist acts during the opening rather than merely receiving exposition.
9. Write two chapters by default:
   - Chapter one establishes the protagonist, personal stake, deadline, and first irreversible disturbance.
   - Chapter two commits the protagonist to action, makes the danger physically real, and ends with a reason to read chapter three.
10. Save UTF-8 Markdown inside a clearly marked Prototype output directory. Include a short provenance note identifying source material and created expansion.
11. Report output paths, approximate character count, sources used, and the largest unverified creative addition. Do not call the draft canon or publication-ready prose unless separately reviewed and accepted.

## Prose rules

- Lead with an event, desire, image, confrontation, or decision. Do not begin with a world encyclopedia.
- Reveal world rules through consequential action: a rule matters when obeying or breaking it changes what happens.
- Prefer concrete objects, bodily action, spatial pressure, dialogue, and sensory evidence over abstract explanation.
- Keep exposition subordinate to the scene. Cut information that the current decision does not need.
- Let characters speak from conflicting wants, not as alternate narrators of the setting manual.
- Vary sentence length and paragraph shape. Avoid repeated aphorisms, symmetrical slogans, rhetorical triples, and repeated “不是……而是……” constructions.
- Do not make every paragraph conclude with an authorial judgment.
- Preserve mystery without withholding the protagonist's immediate objective.
- A non-combat protagonist may guide, interpret, bargain, rescue, build, or choose; do not grant sudden warrior competence merely to service a climax.

## Failure boundaries

- Stop if the world root is ambiguous or the referenced material cannot be read without encoding loss.
- Do not invent a story only by combining file titles. Read the documents supporting the selected protagonist and event.
- Do not let a fact-checking, evidence, contract, governance, or responsibility system become the plot unless a human desire gives it emotional stakes.
- Do not freeze genre or style as a mandatory world constraint. Select them for the current story.
- Do not continue into a full novel, image generation, character gallery, map, comic, production Skill registration, or Renderer integration unless separately requested.
- Do not install this Prototype in a user Skill directory.
